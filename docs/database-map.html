Scan Studio Database Map

One SQLite catalog holds everything the studio knows; pixels are files it knows by checksum. Below: the tables grouped by what they hold, why every part is now an item, and how each thing lines up with a record of the archive site.

The treemap: tables by group

A part is an item

Until now a part was only a region row, and the archival facts of a part (place, number, category) sat on the geometry. Schema 2 gives every sheet region exactly one item of kind part. The region keeps the outline and the edits; the item keeps what the archive cares about, in the same table and under the same database rules as every other thing. A part without a place or category of its own follows its scan; a piece found on a cut follows the part the cut came from.

Trashed with its outlineA part removed in the studio puts its item in the Trash; Undo brings it back, unless its number was taken meanwhile.
Titled by its labelThe part's label is the item's title. The cut file's name is the fallback for the archive.
Numbered like the archiveItems and child places share one number sequence per place; the database refuses a second use.
CheckedLibrary › Check reports a part without its item, two items for one part, or an item out of step with its part.

Two routes, one shape: Package… writes records.json and the cut files into a folder for the archive's importer and works with a read-only token or none. Send to archive posts the same records straight to Directus and needs a token allowed to create records and files.

Rules the database itself enforces

  • Foreign keys on, STRICT tables, JSON columns validated.
  • A place cannot be inside itself; a place that still keeps things cannot be deleted.
  • Two things in one place never share a number (items and child places together); a number needs a place; unknown categories are refused.
  • A part item needs a sheet region and belongs to that region's item; one item per region.
  • Managed files are named by checksum: identical bytes are one file.
